# Reward Function Ablations

Evaluation of reward function modifications across 3 seeds (6M steps each) paired on held-out sets (31000 / 33000 / 39000; 120 buildings each). Baseline: `ppo_carry_s2` (shipped policy).

| Experiment | Win Rate $\Delta$ (Climb) | Win Rate $\Delta$ (Policy Argmax) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Potential shaping ($w=25$) | **-3.4%** | -1.2% |
| 1b. Potential shaping ($w=5$) | **-3.9%** | -1.4% |
| 2. `toon_lost_penalty = 40` | -0.4% | **+2.6%** |
| 3. `horizon_cost = scarcity_cost = 0` | -0.7% | +1.4% |

None of the experimental configurations exceeded the baseline on the live agent (PPO + climb).

## Context and Hypotheses

Building win rate correlates strongly with first-toon survival: intact 4-toon parties win 100% (60/60), while parties with a casualty drop to 7%. The baseline reward uses `toon_lost_penalty = 0.0`, penalizing a loss only via local laff damage ($\approx -20$) and terminal loss penalties delayed until full wipeout.

- **Experiments 1 & 2**: Penalize toon loss earlier.
- **Experiment 3**: Test necessity of `horizon_cost` and `scarcity_cost` (conservation penalties).

## Experiment 1 & 1b: Potential-Based Reward Shaping

Tested potential $\Phi(s) = w \cdot (\text{toons alive})$ applied as $F = \gamma \Phi(s') - \Phi(s)$ (policy invariance under optimal MDP conditions per Ng et al., 1999).

- **Result**: Degraded live agent win rate by 3–4% across all 18 set comparisons ($w=25$ and $w=5$).
- **Mechanism**: `toons_alive` is already an explicit feature in the observation vector. The potential did not supply unobserved state information, but altered the gradient trajectory and value-baseline estimation in finite-sample PPO.

## Experiment 2: Immediate Toon Loss Penalty

Tested fixed penalty `toon_lost_penalty = 40` at the step a toon is eliminated.

- **Result**: Policy argmax improved $+2.6\%$ (positive in 7 of 9 evaluations). When filtered through the coordinate ascent climb, win rate delta was neutral ($-0.4\%$).
- Policy risk aversion did not induce passivity (training return recovered after initial drop).

## Experiment 3: Gag Conservation Terms Ablation

Setting `horizon_cost = scarcity_cost = 0`:

| Metric | Baseline | Conservation Disabled |
|---|---|---|
| Level 4 Gags Thrown | 46.0% | ~34% |
| Level 5 Gags Thrown | 20.9% | ~31% |
| Level 7 Gags Thrown | 3.5% | 3.4–3.5% |
| Total Gags Thrown | 3433 | 3484–3776 |
| Pouch Exhaustions | 0 | **0** |
| Mean Toons Lost | 0.86 | 0.77–0.85 |

- **Outcomes**: Win rate was unaffected (climb $-0.7\%$, argmax $+1.4\%$).
- **Behavior**: Shifts gag selection from level 4 to level 5 without altering encounter outcomes. Pouch exhaustions remained at zero across all seeds. Level 7 usage remained constrained by policy prior rather than scarcity cost.

## Conclusion: Climb Sensitivity vs Policy Capacity

The coordinate ascent climb acts as the primary determinant of live agent performance, remaining within 68–69% across substantial policy variations:

| Modification | Policy Argmax $\Delta$ | Climb $\Delta$ |
|---|---|---|
| Carry-limit retrain | +2.8% | +1.2% |
| `toon_lost_penalty` | +2.6% | -0.4% |
| Conservation terms disabled | +1.4% | -0.7% |
| Survival shaping | -1.4% | -3.9% |
| Track entropy regularization | -4.5% | +1.1% |

Because `_quick_score` re-ranks candidate menus generated by the policy, marginal policy improvements fail to propagate to final agent win rates. Subsequent performance optimizations were redirected to the scoring objective and search mechanics (see [scorer-audit.md](scorer-audit.md)).
